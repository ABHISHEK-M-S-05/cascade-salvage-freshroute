PS 03 FreshRoute: AI-powered perishable recovery and distribution

Where every harvest outruns its expiry.

CASCADE SALVAGE decides where each batch of produce should go while it can still get there: premium retail, a rapid mandi or a processing plant. When a highway jams, it re-plans.

A batch has hours, not days.

Four steps through the problem, using the inventory the API is serving right now.

1 of 4

The clock starts at harvest

Every batch at the Nashik aggregation hub arrives with a shelf life measured in hours. A destination only accepts it if enough of that time is left when the truck pulls in.

    2 of 4

    Conventional dispatch stops at the first no

    The engine's baseline copies common practice: offer stock to the highest-paying retail and mandi buyers, never to processors. Stock that is rejected on arrival, or that nobody takes, expires.

    3 of 4

    Cascade to the next feasible buyer

    CASCADE SALVAGE checks every destination a batch can still reach, then cascades through three tiers.

    • Premium retail takes grade A stock that still meets the store's minimum shelf life after the trip.
    • Rapid mandi moves time-sensitive stock fast, with a 10% or 15% velocity discount when the buffer is tight.
    • Processing plant is tried first for batches with 20 hours or less, turning near-expiry stock into pulp, puree or frozen product.

    Each batch goes to one destination. Whatever that destination cannot take is counted as unallocated.

    4 of 4

    When the highway jams, re-plan

    A jam adds hours to a route's transit time. Batches that were feasible may now arrive with too little shelf life left, so the engine runs again and finds the next option, or reports honestly that there is none.

    From boiling point to deep freeze

    The thermal path of a leafy-green batch rescued into processing: blanched near 100 °C, quenched in ice water, then frozen to −18 °C so it keeps for months instead of hours.

    Illustrative curve. CASCADE SALVAGE models shelf life in hours and has no temperature sensors. These are typical processing values, not measurements.

      Values follow common practice: greens are blanched about 2 minutes in boiling water and chilled in ice-cold water (USU Extension); the food-safety danger zone is 40–140 °F, about 4–60 °C (USDA FSIS); −18 °C (0 °F) is the usual frozen-storage temperature.

          What this prototype does not claim
          
            
            
              
          • No carbon or emissions figures: the data has no fuel or vehicle-load fields.
            • 
              
          • No measured food waste: spoilage here is stock the model cannot place, not a physical count.
            • 
              
          • No accounting profit: the engine's net_profit field is revenue minus transport, so this page calls it contribution after transport.
            • 
              
          • No live tracking or temperature telemetry: routes have a status text field only.
            • 
              
          • Vehicle capacity is not checked: routes list a vehicle type but no numeric capacity.
            • 
            
          
          
          
          
          
            
          Context from outside the model
          
            
          A post-harvest loss study commissioned by India's Ministry of Food Processing Industries and run by NABCONS (reference years 2020–22) covered 54 agricultural commodities. It put total tomato loss at 11.62% (FreshPlaza) and annual losses at 7.36 million tonnes of fruit and 11.97 million tonnes of vegetables (Lok Sabha reply). These figures describe the national problem; they are not outputs of this model.
